# Chapter 9 · Multimodality and Real-Time Interaction — Knowledge Point Extraction

> Source: `book/chapter9.md`. Focus on the three scenarios where **real-time constraints make multimodal problems harder** — voice dialogue, GUI operation (Computer Use), and robot control. Three shared threads: **multimodal perception + extreme latency sensitivity → from serial pipelines toward end-to-end models**; running through the whole chapter are the **fast/slow thinking collaboration** and the **world model (predicting consequences)**.

## 1. Voice: The Most Natural Human-Machine Interface

### [KP-09-01] The value of voice and architecture evolution: the coordinate system of three paradigms
- **Core in one sentence**: Voice is the highest-bandwidth, most natural human-machine interface (speaking is about four times faster than typing and doesn't occupy the hands or eyes); the architecture evolution is summarized by the three-way classification OpenAI used when releasing GPT-Live in 2026 — Cascaded / end-to-end all-modal Omni / Full-Duplex — and the thread running through it all is "**how to shed the turn-taking assumption and shed VAD's guessing at turns**".
- **Key design points**: Two kinds of voice products (voice input method vs voice Agent); the advanced usage **whisper coding** (dictating to command a coding/research Agent; this book was written this way); cascade = ASR→LLM→TTS serial (information lost in transmission, slow and stiff responses); end-to-end all-modal Omni = a single model listening, speaking, and thinking as one (low latency that preserves prosody and emotion, **but still assumes turn-taking**, relying on VAD to judge turns and prone to talking over the user); full-duplex = listening and speaking at the same time, making decisions several times per second on "should I speak / listen / stop / interrupt / call a tool" (**completely removing the turn-taking assumption**; the 2024 Moshi research was the opening note, 2026 GPT-Live with 150 million users); the three paradigms **coexist long-term** rather than new-replacing-old; GPT-Live's second structural change = **decoupling** "real-time interaction" from "deep thinking" (delegating complex problems to background frontier models).
- **Maka design implications**: voice is a natural candidate for the future multimodal entry point; designing real-time voice interaction requires first clarifying the "turn model" assumption; "fast/slow decoupling" is highly isomorphic with TaskRun/continuation (foreground fast interaction keeps the conversation alive while background slow tasks keep running); Log is the Runtime can log all the events of VAD / turns / interruptions / delegation.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Multimodal Interface Overall Architecture (Voice / Vision / Operation) Design"

### [KP-09-02] Cascaded pipelines: modularity, latency accumulation, and end-to-end streaming
- **Core in one sentence**: Most commercial voice assistants use the VAD→ASR→LLM→TTS serial pipeline (total latency around 0.95–2.3 seconds); production systems rely on **end-to-end streaming + preemptive/speculative generation** to squeeze the latency down.
- **Key design points**: per-stage latency (VAD silence threshold of 500–800ms — too short truncates sentences, too long idles doing nothing; ASR 50–500ms; LLM TTFT 100–500ms, 5–10 seconds after reasoning; TTS 200–500ms); **queueing latency** (total latency ≈ idle latency × 1/(1−utilization) — at 50% utilization latency doubles, at 80% it is 5×, so servers cannot stay at high load for long); the end-to-end streaming trio (ASR transcribes while listening, but at turn end the **final text must be confirmed** (provisional transcripts can be rewritten); the LLM streams output cut by punctuation and semantics into playable small segments, handing the first segment to TTS as soon as it forms; TTS synthesizes incrementally and starts as soon as it receives the first segment); **preemptive/speculative generation** (transcribe the more stable parts and start the LLM early, cancel/restart/correct when the transcription changes, and synthesize ahead but wait for turn confirmation before playing) — whether it really overlaps depends on **explicitly implementing partial-transcript commit, invalidation, and rollback mechanisms**, not on merely flipping a stream switch.
- **Maka design implications**: the tool-calling chain is equally serial (model → tool → result → re-reasoning), and the latency waterfall and queueing theory can be directly analogized to Headless task scheduling (avoid high-utilization nonlinear latency); "streaming + preemption + provisional results must be confirmed" corresponds to the **Provisional/Confirmed dual fact** — Log is the Runtime should be able to distinguish "provisional transcript / provisional result" from "confirmed fact", preventing provisional states from being consumed as final facts.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Real-Time Pipeline Streaming and Latency Budget Design (Including Provisional/Confirmed Evidence Semantics)"

### [KP-09-03] Streaming voice perception: replacing VAD + ASR
- **Core in one sentence**: The traditional VAD+ASR cascade has three fundamental problems (latency accumulation, information loss, accuracy degradation); LLM-based streaming auditory perception models merge "recognition and understanding" into a single model, letting the model see "the complete context from the start of the conversation to the current moment".
- **Key design points**: The three fundamental problems (VAD must wait for 500–800ms of silence because it cannot foresee the future; VAD only outputs a speech/silence binary — emotion / tone / hesitation / environmental sounds are all lost; segmenting breaks context continuity — in "john dot smith", smith gets misrecognized as "miss"); **the technical meaning of "streaming"** (causal or chunked encoder plus incremental decoding; the root cause of Whisper not being able to stream = its encoder needs the full audio segment); LLM-based streaming auditory perception (incremental latency of tens to one or two hundred milliseconds, able to see the full context to do **In-Context Learning** and inherit world knowledge and common-sense reasoning — "苹果" (apple) + "发布会" (product launch) → Apple); **acoustic event special markers** (`<speak_start/end>` / `<interrupt>` / `<emotion>` / `<laugh>` / `<noise>` form a unified event stream together with text tokens); the key diagnosis: the root of model flapping is often that **the training labels are "god's-eye view" targets** (labels drawn from audio beyond the decision point) — after relabeling with "only the information available at the decision moment", the spurious flapping disappears — **data matters more than architecture**.
- **Maka design implications**: The four creeds map directly — "Context is not history" requires user evidence (voice/behavior) to preserve the full context rather than being chopped up by VAD; "Log is the Runtime" requires recording user behavior as **structured event markers** (hesitation / interruption / affirmation / emotion) instead of binary signals; if Maka does voice input, it should prefer semantic-level turn judgment over silence thresholds.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Perception-Layer Event Stream and Structured Observation Format Design (VAD → Semantic Turns)"

### [KP-09-04] End-to-end all-modal models (Omni) and "end-to-end vs self-cascade"
- **Core in one sentence**: Omni uses a single model to listen, speak, and think as one, letting the latent space transmit para-verbal information beyond text; **the end-to-end advantage lies mainly in latency — accuracy does not necessarily win** — and the end-to-end vs self-cascade pros and cons can be judged in advance based on the nature of the task.
- **Key design points**: the tradeoff (cascade is modular, independently tunable, and interpretable vs end-to-end has low latency and preserves non-textual information but needs large amounts of training data and has poor interpretability); **self-cascade** (the same model first transcribes into structured text and then reasons over the text — when the answer is mainly determined by semantic content, self-cascade is comparable or better; when the answer depends heavily on non-verbal cues (tone / emotion / environmental sounds), end-to-end is clearly better); **design principle: the key is not whether an intermediate-representation "bottleneck" is introduced, but what information the bottleneck carries** — upgrading the intermediate text into a structured representation that carries para-verbal markers narrows the end-to-end advantage; examples (OpenAI Realtime API, Gemini Live API, Qwen3-Omni Thinker-Talker, MiniCPM-o 4.5 local 9B, Step-Audio 2); experiment 9-4 (end-to-end vs self-cascade scored the same overall but failed at opposite positions — **do not equate end-to-end with more accurate**).
- **Maka design implications**: the "information carried by the bottleneck" principle is extremely critical for Maka — if LLM Compaction / Tool Result pruning degenerates into plain-text summarization, it is creating an information bottleneck; the compression method should be decided by "whether that information carries content the task needs" (preserve structured fields / para-verbal markers).
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Multimodal Perception Technology Selection: End-to-End vs Modular vs Self-Cascade (Including the Intermediate-Representation Information Content Principle)"

### [KP-09-05] Full-duplex interaction model: removing the "turn-taking" assumption
- **Core in one sentence**: Full-duplex carries "getting rid of VAD" to its logical endpoint — removing the "turn-taking" assumption and letting the model listen and speak simultaneously and continuously; interactivity is no longer an external harness wrapped around the model, but **built into the model itself (micro-turns)**.
- **Key design points**: the fundamental challenge of the turn-taking model (simultaneous interpretation and rhythm games require listening / thinking / acting at the same time, while the turn-taking premise forces the three to be separated into sequential time slices); **Moshi** (models two audio streams in parallel plus an "inner monologue" text stream; overlapping speech and interrupting at any moment become natural behavior, with end-to-end latency around 200ms); Thinking Machines Lab — "**if interactivity is to scale with intelligence, interactivity must become part of the model itself**" — **micro-turns** (segments of about 200ms continuously "reading in 200ms, generating 200ms", with multiple streams interleaving forward; the granularity is a deliberate compromise: fine enough that silence / overlap / interruption are preserved as a continuous stream with no artificial turn boundaries, and coarse enough to chunk and process multiple modality streams concurrently to suppress latency); **how slow thinking is attached** (the interaction model only keeps the conversation online and delegates to a stronger background reasoning model — what it hands over is not an isolated query but **the entire conversation context**); GPT-Live (continuously processes input and continuously generates output, waits quietly without talking over when the user is thinking, gives "嗯/对" (mhm / right) affirmations to signal it is listening, handles real-time translation well, and divides fast/slow labor by delegating to background GPT-5.5); the endpoint of the narrative arc (VAD guessing switches by silence → the streaming-perception semantic layer → full-duplex completely dissolving the switches, so most of the barge-in handling chain is architecturally saved).
- **Maka design implications**: "Task may outlive a Turn" and full-duplex's "foreground keeps the conversation alive + background slow reasoning delegation" are the same structure; "pass the whole context when delegating" suggests the background task start protocol should carry the full context rather than an isolated instruction; the micro-turn idea can be analogized to Maka continuously observing multiple input streams (keyboard / tool results / system events) rather than being turn-driven.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Real-Time Interaction Layer: Foreground Interaction Stream and Background Task (TaskRun) Decoupling Design"

## 2. Tradeoffs in Thinking Architecture and the Fast/Slow Interface

### [KP-09-06] Fast/slow thinking tradeoffs: Option One (fast handling, slow answering) and Option Two (fast interaction, slow reminding)
- **Core in one sentence**: The fundamental contradiction between real-time response and deep thinking admits three tradeoffs; Option One "fast handling, slow answering" has two big problems — overthinking and fast/slow inconsistency; Option Two "fast interaction, slow reminding" (slow thinking as the "military advisor") became the frontier mainstream in 2026, yet still suffers the essential limitations that "fast thinking does not follow orders, cannot expose intermediate thinking, and cannot think while speaking".
- **Key design points**: Options One and Two are "two independent models running concurrently" — the fast/slow division of labor does not depend on end-to-end; **Option One** (fast thinking answers briefly within 500ms while slow thinking runs 5–10 seconds of test-time scaling in the background and then gives a complete answer) — Problem 1 overthinking ("what day is it today" — fast thinking already answered correctly while slow thinking still runs 10 seconds), Problem 2 fast/slow inconsistency (two independent thinking processes may contradict each other and shatter trust); **Option Two** (slow thinking can "see" fast thinking's output, provides suggestions through an **Agent status bar** rather than speaking directly to the user, and keeps thinking during speaking gaps — production instances: GPT-Live delegation / Pine AI / Grok Voice / Thinking Machines Lab); **the pragmatic reason for decoupling** (frontier reasoning models iterate every few months, real-time interaction needs specially designed training objectives, and stuffing both into the same model means chasing a moving target plus diluting the most precious reasoning capability — instead, only train a lightweight foreground interaction model and keep the strongest reasoning model untouched in the background, so you always use the current strongest "brain"); **Option Two's essential limitations** (①fast thinking may not follow orders — communication is indirect and fuzzy ②intermediate thinking results are inaccessible ("two people solving a problem together can only pass notes; they cannot see each other's scratch paper") ③the fundamental theoretical problem: **"thinking while speaking" cannot be achieved**).
- **Maka design implications**: TaskRun/continuation + Event Log naturally support Option Two — the slow Task runs in the background and produces a **structured event stream** consumed by the foreground UI, rather than a single natural-language suggestion; beware "fast thinking not following orders" — background task results should be returned as structured events (goal / progress / evidence / pending-confirmation items) instead of free text; Creed 4 correspondence: a background slow conclusion cannot automatically become a system fact — it can only be upgraded after foreground/user confirmation.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Fast/Slow Decoupling: Event Contract Design Between the Foreground Interaction Layer and Background Tasks"

### [KP-09-07] Option Three: end-to-end unification of thinking and expression (Step-Audio R1 "thinking while speaking")
- **Core in one sentence**: To truly achieve "thinking while speaking" you must internalize thinking ability directly into the end-to-end audio model; Step-Audio R1 uses two complementary mechanisms — **MGRD (modality-anchored thinking distillation)** first solves "whether the thinking is right", and the **MPS dual-brain architecture** then solves "whether the speaking is timely".
- **Key design points**: the background problem "**Textual Surrogate Reasoning**" (the longer an audio model's thinking chain, the worse its performance — it reasons semantically over the text transcription rather than analyzing acoustic features; the root cause = in the training data the CoT is generated by text models, so pure-text thinking patterns are inherited); **MGRD's three-step iteration** (generate multiple thoughts and filter out the ones truly based on acoustic features → retrain on the high-quality thinking data → use RL to prevent laziness that skips thinking and guesses directly); **MPS dual-brain architecture, Mind-Paced Speaking** (the conceiving brain continuously thinks and produces thought-result segments, while the expressing brain converts each received segment into speech combined with previous thinking, with the two pipelined in parallel — t=0 conceiving analysis → t=200ms outputs the first segment → t=350ms the user hears the first syllable); the cost and the industry divide (Option Three chases the "moving target" and must be retrained repeatedly; frontier products mostly bet on Option Two decoupling — **the tradeoff between "a swappable brain" and "tighter thinking-while-speaking" is not about who replaces whom**).
- **Maka design implications**: MGRD's "modality mismatch" diagnosis is general for Maka — if Maka "textualizes" tool results / screen frames / logs as reasoning input, beware "textual surrogate" distortion; "thinking while speaking" inspires the UI to incrementally present intermediate states as background tasks advance (event-stream projection) rather than only waiting for the terminal state; Log is the Runtime supports recording thinking intermediate states as events for playback and audit.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Event-Based Expression and Incremental Presentation of Thinking Intermediate States"

### [KP-09-08] The interface between fast and slow: Latent Bridge (what else can be passed beyond text)
- **Core in one sentence**: In Option Two, passing suggestions between fast and slow through the text channel is only a "thin straw"; real-time game evidence shows **Latent Bridge** is feasible — freeze both the fast and slow models and train only a small "bridge" that projects the slow model's hidden-layer conclusions into latent tokens spliced into the fast model's input; it also gives an honest boundary: **whether fast/slow collaboration helps depends on whether the task bottleneck is "being able to think of it" or "being able to react in time"**.
- **Key design points**: scenario = real-time games (the fast model makes dozens of actions per second, the slow model thinks once per second, both models are frozen, and only a small bridge of a few tens of millions of parameters is trained to project the slow model's hidden-layer conclusions into a few "latent tokens" spliced into the fast input); results (across multiple Atari games, +26% to +82% over the traditional text channel, at a cost of about 5ms extra per step); **the honest boundary** (the bridge only helps when the slow thinking is genuinely stronger than the fast reaction — cross-game correlation r≈0.9; on purely reaction-speed tasks no bridge helps); cross-scenario preview (Computer Use will face the same problem — when is it worth inviting the "slow military advisor"); echoes the Agent status bar (Option Two's production instance's "pure-text contract", which is exactly the Agent status bar this book has described from Chapter 2 all the way to now).
- **Maka design implications**: Maka is a text/file-oriented Agent, and the "text channel" is the low-cost default; the quality of fast/slow collaboration depends on **interface information bandwidth** — prefer background tasks to pass **structured events** (goal / progress / evidence / pending-confirmation items) to the foreground rather than a single natural-language suggestion; under Log is the Runtime, what the interface passes should be "log projections" rather than throwaway text.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Fast/Slow Interface Contract: Structured Events vs Text Summaries vs Latent-Space Channels"

### [KP-09-09] More human-like speech synthesis: control-marker-driven multimodal TTS
- **Core in one sentence**: Traditional TTS's "perfection" is exactly the problem (too fluent, zero pauses — obviously a machine at first listen); human pauses / filler words / occasional repetitions are the natural externalization of the thinking process; the solution is to hand the decision authority over "where to pause, what tone" to the main LLM, and drive multimodal TTS with control markers.
- **Key design points**: human "imperfection" is a signal (pauses / filler words "嗯/呃/那个" (mhm / uh / um) / occasional repetition convey to the listener "I'm thinking" or "I'm not quite sure"); the solution = the LLM outputs text plus control markers (`[THINKING]` a 1–2 second thinking pause with filler sound, `[SEARCHING]` a shorter pause with searching filler, `[EMO:happy]` adjusts tone and prosody, `[SPEED:0.8x]` controls speaking rate — only the LLM knows when to pause, speed up, or be lively); TTS plays the role of **multimodal generator** (input text + control markers → output audio, with control markers generating non-verbal audio `[SIGH]` / `[LAUGH:small]` / `[BREATH]`); two implementation paths (a self-developed TTS with native support vs **voice cloning** — prepare dozens of reference utterances with different emotions / rates / styles for the same virtual persona and, per marker, select the best match to drive a TTS API, deployable within weeks); experiment 9-5 (Fish Audio S1 voice cloning: a 24-reference voice library, three-configuration comparison — the multi-reference library comes closest to a real human customer-service agent).
- **Maka design implications**: in future voice output, "human-like" pauses and fillers are interaction signals, not defects; the control-marker idea is transferable — Maka's Agent output can carry **intent/confidence markers** (confirming / searching / thinking / uncertain) for the frontend to render and downstream systems to judge; a Creed 4 extension — expression-layer uncertainty markers ("I'm not quite sure") should not be treated as facts; uncertainty and evidence should both go into the log.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Expression-Layer Output Markers and Confidence Expression Design"

## 3. Computer Use: GUI Automation Agents

### [KP-09-10] The Computer Use perception-thinking-action loop and action space design
- **Core in one sentence**: Computer Use lets AI use software like a human by observing the screen and operating the mouse and keyboard; the core is the **perception-thinking-action loop**; the action space can be cleanly divided into three tool categories — GUI operations / command execution / file editing — and it is not a model-vendor-private protocol.
- **Key design points**: the four-step loop (screenshot → the multimodal model receives the screenshot plus the task and outputs thinking and actions → the execution layer executes → wait for the UI to respond and screenshot again); three key design dimensions (**action space** / visual grounding / model architecture); the action space (Anthropic's reference implementation has three tools: **computer tool** for GUI operations (mouse / scroll / keyboard type, character-by-character at 12ms to simulate real typing, and the perception actions screenshot / cursor_position / wait), **bash tool** for command execution (persistent bash session, 120-second timeout, sentinel-string detection, environment state maintained across calls), **str_replace_editor** for file editing (string-matching safe edits)); **the action space is not a private protocol** (as long as the Harness can convert the same screenshots / action constraints / execution results into the messages and structured outputs the target model supports, Claude / open-weight / self-hosted models can all drive the same loop).
- **Maka design implications**: Maka is a text/file-oriented Agent and its tool calls are its action space; extending to Computer Use directly reuses the "action space = structured tool contract" idea (each category of action is a tool, not free text); "any model can drive the action space" supports a multi-backend design (swapping models does not require rewriting the action layer); Log is the Runtime records every screen action as an event, supporting playback, audit, and recovery.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Computer Use Action Space and Tool Contract Design"

### [KP-09-11] Computer Use visual grounding: three routes
- **Core in one sentence**: In every loop the model must accurately locate the target element in the screenshot (Grounding); there are two big ideas — **multiple choice** (label elements with numbers and let the model choose, including pure-visual Set-of-Mark labeling and structured element indexing) and **pure coordinate prediction**; coordinate prediction requires special handling of **resolution matching and bidirectional coordinate scaling**.
- **Key design points**: the shared advantage of the multiple-choice idea (it turns the open-ended "find the button, predict the coordinate" into the closed-form "pick one from the already-labeled elements" — like multiple choice being easier than fill-in-the-blank); **Set-of-Mark SoM** (Microsoft 2023, to unlock GPT-4V: a segmentation model automatically cuts candidate regions and overlays numbers, and the model reports a number that is converted to the element's center coordinate — no DOM needed, applicable to native desktops/games); **structured element indexing** (browser-use: CDP obtains the DOM tree and accessibility information → auto-detect interactive elements → assign unique IDs and draw bounding boxes → generate a text list — it doesn't save tokens but grounding is accurate and stable); **pure coordinate prediction** (SeeClick / Claude computer use: trained on massive GUI screenshots plus element-position pairs to learn the mapping); **the resolution problem** (a model's coordinate understanding depends on its training resolution (Claude uses XGA 1024×768 / WXGA / 1366×768); when the input doesn't match, coordinates are systematically offset; the tool layer must implement **bidirectional coordinate scaling** and **choose the target resolution by aspect ratio** (2560×1440 → pick the 16:9 1366×768, and the model's output (683,384) is reverse-mapped to (1280,720)); hard-stretching into 4:3 squashes the image and offsets coordinates); the selection logic (**when structured information is available → prefer DOM/Accessibility Tree indexing**; when it isn't (native desktops / Canvas / games) → visual labeling or coordinate prediction; both have accuracy gaps on small elements and dense interfaces).
- **Maka design implications**: Grounding is the key to Computer Use success; when doing GUI operations, choose the route by "structured information availability" (browser DOM indexing, native apps falling back to visual/coordinate), and **aspect-ratio-matched bidirectional coordinate scaling is a must**; grounding evidence (screenshot + element ID list) should go into the log — "Context is not history" requires the grounding basis to be reconstructable rather than relying on one-off visual context.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Visual Grounding and Coordinate Scaling Design (SoM / DOM / Coordinate Three Routes)"

### [KP-09-12] Can see animation, can hear sound: the Agent-Computer Observation Interface (AOI)
- **Core in one sentence**: Traditional Computer Use implicitly assumes "the screen is static" (a screenshot every 3–5 seconds, and no ears); what truly needs redesigning is not the action interface but the **observation interface** — decouple continuous, adaptive, multimodal **observation** from discrete **actions**, making it a perception middleware (AOI) inserted between the environment and any off-the-shelf Computer Use model, **with no retraining required**.
- **Key design points**: three "open the valve on demand" components (①**inter-frame keyframe capture** — an extremely cheap pixel gate skips frames that haven't changed, plus a small model judges meaningful changes and only captures frames on change ②**volume-gated speech transcription** — speech recognition is invoked only when there is sound, giving the Agent ears ③**the most critical: narrate the screen into persistent text** — each captured frame is described in one sentence, so **even if the original image is later cleaned out of the context, this text remains in memory**); what works is not "which frames to select" but "narrating frames into text that persists long-term" — text is the modality LLM Agents are best at; the middleware brings +17 to +48pp across eight models without retraining, with the largest gap on speech-type tasks; it is not a fixed configuration (too many image tokens in some models actually crowd out reasoning — components are **selected per model rather than all switched on** — **there is no silver bullet in perception solutions; configure them following the model's temperament**).
- **Maka design implications**: this is the most direct embodiment of "Context is not history" — dynamic visual information should enter the context as **persistent text narration**, even if the original frames are pruned (pruning removes frames, not the knowledge narrated out of them); decoupling observation from actions, on-demand valve-opening, and eventized logging are highly isomorphic — **only changes worth recording produce events**; when Maka does Computer Use in the future, AOI serves as a "perception-as-a-service" middleware.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Observation Interface (AOI): Keyframe Gating and Persistent Text Narration Design"

### [KP-09-13] The Computer Use world model: from screenshot loop to predictable desktop
- **Core in one sentence**: The "screenshot → think → click" loop can be formulated as a discrete-environment world-model problem (desktop state + action → next-state representation), letting the Agent compare candidate action consequences before real clicks, predict the next step during loading, recover from popup differences, and run **counterfactual experiments** on isolated virtual desktops (2026 Photon-1 calls it an *imagination model*).
- **Key design points**: desktop state is not just pixels (windows / focus / scroll / input boxes / loading / permissions / network); the benefits (compare candidate action consequences before real clicks / predict the next step during page loading / recover from fleeting popups via state differences / avoid blocking every round on "re-screenshot and re-understand the whole image"); **counterfactual experiments** (on an isolated virtual desktop, first simulate "click delete"; if it predicts an irreversible confirmation box, request user confirmation rather than directly deleting the real file); **Photon-1** (pretraining does not generate screenshots pixel-by-pixel; each frame is compressed into discrete latent tokens and the next state is predicted autoregressively; pretraining needs no action labels, fewer than 35,000 trajectories teach the action format, and online RL optimizes it; trained on 30,000 H200 hours with a compute cost under $100k; the extra image generator is only for visualization and **is not a necessary component for inference**); **the key is not "generating more realistic screenshots" but first predicting the next state needed to complete the task, then generating the action that brings the current state to that state** (after execution, only check whether the real state matches the predicted key differences).
- **Maka design implications**: the world model = "comparable predictions", a mirror image of Creed 4 — predictions/simulations are not facts and must be validated against the real state after execution; "predict-confirm-execute" counterfactual checks can be applied directly to Maka dangerous actions (file deletion) — a stronger safety mechanism than "I've checked"; the log records the "predicted state vs actual state" difference as validation evidence, forming a self-healing loop.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Desktop World Model and Action-Consequence Prediction (Including Counterfactual Confirmation for Dangerous Operations)"

### [KP-09-14] Two deployment challenges for Computer Use: mobile ecosystem barriers and real-time performance
- **Core in one sentence**: Mobile technical differences are not the main obstacle; what really blocks things is the **ecosystem barrier** (Agent operations bypass the traffic- and attention-based monetization model, structurally conflicting with platform business models); Computer Use's **real-time performance** still has no systematic solution, but **fast/slow decoupling can "hide the slowness"**.
- **Key design points**: mobile technical differences (accessing the system accessibility-service API to read elements and dispatch clicks/input; mouse coordinates become touch gestures, requiring extra gesture-type definitions); **the ecosystem barrier** (phone vendors integrating AI assistants that automatically operate WeChat / Taobao / Alipay face platform restrictions — traditional internet monetization relies on traffic and attention (feed ads / recommendations / impulse purchases); when an Agent operates on the user's behalf, the AI watches no ads, makes no impulse purchases, and goes straight for the goal, so every operation erodes the platform's monetization foundation — a structural conflict of interest that is hard to reconcile in the short term); **real-time performance** (the OSWorld-Human efficiency study: even successful Agents take clearly more steps than humans, and **per-step reasoning latency keeps growing as the task advances** (the longer the context, the slower the decisions) — "reaching human-level accuracy does not equal being practical; efficiency is the real bottleneck"); the root of inefficiency (the serial "screenshot-think-click" loop accumulates latency, plus a complete inability to "think ahead" — if you predict the next step while executing the current action, thinking and execution can overlap); **the proven detour (fast/slow decoupling)** (a small, fast model handles real-time voice dialogue while a frontier VLM operates the browser slowly, step by step, communicating through a minimal "**pure-text contract**" — the slow Agent attaches a one-sentence rolling status summary to each operation ("filling the form, still need your date of birth"), the fast Agent answers in real time based on it and relays the user's new spoken information back to the slow Agent, and **the fast Agent must never say "it's done" before the status summary confirms completion**; the decoupling made voice responses about **15× faster** (0.58s vs 8.64s) with no drop in success rate; remove the text channel and the success rate instantly collapses to 0 — this "pure-text contract" is essentially the Agent status bar).
- **Maka design implications**: the ecosystem barrier reminds Maka — **the value of a local-first desktop Agent lies precisely in not depending on the platform monetization chain**, sidestepping the mobile ecosystem risk; for real-time performance, adopt "fast/slow decoupling + status-summary contract" as the default Computer Use interaction architecture (slow TaskRun + fast foreground UI feedback); "must not say it's done before confirmation" is a direct implementation of Creed 4 — **the task-completion fact is defined by terminal-state events, not by foreground claims**.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Computer Use Real-Time Performance: Fast/Slow Decoupling and 'Pure-Text Contract' Status Summary Design"

## 4. Robot Manipulation

### [KP-09-15] The hierarchical structure of robot control
- **Core in one sentence**: Robot systems are layered by different time scales (task goal → long-horizon planning → basic skills → VLA/skill policies → low-level control and safety layer); hardware is usually already sufficient for basic manipulation, so first test "what a sufficiently smart operator can do" and then measure the autonomous system's gap.
- **Key design points**: four questions and four technologies each doing their own job (long-horizon planning arranges the order, VLA completes the current skill, the world model estimates consequences, Sim2Real handles training-vs-reality differences); **hardware/algorithm division of labor** (low-cost robots can already complete grasp-place-wipe via teleoperation — first measure the "sufficiently smart operator ceiling" (experiment 9-8: desktop simulator, 10,240 episodes, 100% success even in the worst-case conditions) and then see how far the autonomous system is); the control layering table (task goal minute-level → long-horizon planning seconds to minutes → basic skills about 1–3 seconds → VLA/skill policies 1–10 Hz → low-level control 50–1000 Hz); **engineering division of labor rather than a single model architecture** — no matter how it is implemented, "task ordering" and "immediate action" must be separated (otherwise high-level reasoning latency drags down low-level control); **long-horizon planning and task decomposition** (the planner first lists objects and goals to fix the order, and writes clear start/completion conditions and risk constraints for each step; after each skill completes you obtain a **checkable node** — if grasping fails, only the current step is retried; if an object gets moved, only the affected steps are re-planned without redoing the old plan); the tools given to the agent should be simple enough (**one call does one thing, a fixed action scope, timeout limits, and re-observe immediately after execution** — experiment 9-9 opened only five tools with clear responsibilities and fixed permissions).
- **Maka design implications**: the layering idea is isomorphic with the TaskRun hierarchy (task → subtask → tool call → low-level execution) — decouple planning from execution; "first test the ideal ceiling, then compare the gap" is sound methodology for designing evaluation; checkable nodes / local failure retry / incremental re-planning are exactly the engineering implementation of "Task may outlive a Turn".
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Hierarchical Task Scheduling and Planning Decomposition (Drawing on Robot Control, Including Checkable Nodes)"

### [KP-09-16] VLA control and its limitations
- **Core in one sentence**: VLA (vision-language-action) models receive the current view plus a skill instruction and output actions; action representations come in two kinds — discrete tokens and continuous trajectories — and in engineering **action chunking** hides reasoning latency inside execution time; but VLA has four limitations: limited training data, imitation without understanding consequences, robots differing from one another, and possibly stale observations.
- **Key design points**: definition (current observation + skill instruction → action); the action-representation tradeoff (RT-2 / OpenVLA discrete tokens vs π₀ continuous smooth trajectories — **the real tradeoff is in how actions are represented, not just model size**); **action chunking** (the model reasons 1–10 times per second vs a traditional controller dozens to thousands of times per second — the model generates a short segment of future actions at once, a control thread executes at high frequency, and the model prepares the next segment in the background, hiding the reasoning wait inside execution time; the cost = the longer the action segment, the smoother, but the fewer new frames the model sees in that time window — **a tradeoff between smoothness and reaction speed, not a free speedup** (π₀ typically 25–50 future actions at 50Hz)); **the four limitations** (①limited training data (robot demonstrations are far scarcer than internet text/images) ②it only learns to imitate, not necessarily to understand consequences (behavior cloning learns "what the demonstrator does next" without being asked "what result this action causes") ③robots differ from each other (degrees of freedom / coordinate systems / grippers / latency differ) ④**observations can be stale** (once an action chunk starts executing, objects may be moved or occluded while the model still decides based on the previous frame)); the conclusion: a language model knowing "cup" does not mean it knows friction / contact / liquid sloshing; **VLA mainly answers "what should be done now"; you still need a world model to judge "what might happen after doing it"**.
- **Maka design implications**: action chunking = hiding reasoning latency inside execution time, isomorphic with Maka's asynchronous tool execution / streaming results — one reasoning pass can first submit a segment of actions and then asynchronously refresh the observation; "observations can be stale" warns Maka that in-flight evidence (files modified externally / network-state changes) can invalidate planned actions, requiring re-observation after execution (echoing verify_state and Creed 4).
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Action Generation and Execution Decoupling: Chunked Execution and Observation Refresh Strategy"

### [KP-09-17] The robot world model (action-result predictor)
- **Core in one sentence**: The world model = an action-result predictor (current state + candidate action → predict next state → compare candidate results → choose the action / re-plan / stop safely); **"can generate video" ≠ "can guide robot actions"**.
- **Key design points**: definition (learn "how the state changes in the next instant when a given action is taken in the current state"); a world model usable for robots must do at least three things (understand the current state / predict the likely results of different actions / hand the predictions to the planner to help it choose); a VLM that only describes video or only generates images does not automatically become a reliable world model (it must also know what actions are, and predict actions' effects on objects and the environment — V-JEPA 2 / World-Action Model); **three uses** (①before acting — compare candidate actions and prefer the lower-risk one ②during execution — compare real observations with predictions, and on divergence shorten the action / stop / re-plan ③during training — learn state changes from video / simulation / failure trajectories to reduce real-machine trial and error); the honest boundary (the world model gives a **comparable prediction** of "what might happen if you do this", not a definite answer; the farther the prediction, the larger the error; even a realistic future video may not conform to real contact/friction laws; real systems still need short-term prediction / real-time observation / uncertainty estimation / **an independent hardware safety controller**).
- **Maka design implications**: the world model's "comparable prediction rather than fact" is exactly the mirror of Creed 4 — self-checks and predictions can only produce candidates and evidence, and must not automatically upgrade to system facts; "during execution compare real observation vs prediction, and on divergence shorten / stop / re-plan" is Maka's safe-execution template (aborting dangerous operations early); the log records both the "prediction" and the "actual" as two facts so they can be compared and reviewed.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Action-Consequence Prediction and Divergence Detection (World Model Mode) Design"

### [KP-09-18] From simulation to reality (Sim2Real): cross-environment difference management
- **Core in one sentence**: Sim2Real is not about swapping controllers but about handling the differences between the training environment and the real environment; **the more variation the model sees during training, the more likely it is to adapt to new images**, but more variation is not always better, and "similar tests / unfamiliar tests / real-machine results" should be reported separately.
- **Key design points**: the difference checklist (background / lighting / camera position / object material / friction coefficient / sensor noise / actuator latency — **when the differences are large enough, the actions learned during training fail in reality**); experiment 9-10 (RGB vision policies across environments: fixed-scene training environment 93.0%; with full randomization the two test environments reach 76.1% / 80.2%, both higher than the fixed-scene 54.8% / 56.5% — validating "the more variation seen during training, the more likely the model adapts"); **more variation is not always better** (too small a range doesn't cover reality, too large means it can't learn a stable target; before deployment, first calibrate cameras / latency / key physical parameters with real data, then gradually expand the training variation range); **report "tests similar to training images" / "unfamiliar test scenes" / "real-machine results" separately**; this chapter's closing loop (task goal → long-horizon planning → basic skills → VLA/skill policies → low-level control → real observation and checking → continue / retry / stop / re-plan — the four technologies combine within a single system, each solving a different problem, **not four models replacing one another**).
- **Maka design implications**: Sim2Real's "data diversity determines generalization" has universal meaning for Maka tool validation / multi-environment adaptation — an adapter or tool behavior validated in a development environment does not necessarily hold on a real desktop/browser; do **cross-environment graded reporting** (similar / unfamiliar / real) rather than single-environment self-certification; "first calibrate real parameters, then gradually expand variation" corresponds to first calibrating against real scenarios (real DOM / coordinates / latency) and then expanding; **real observation verification takes priority over any simulation/simulated evidence**.
- **Writable design doc**: "Maka Multi-Environment Validation and Sim2Real-Style Difference Management Design"

## Golden Quotes of This Chapter / Core Conclusions
- "A voice pause longer than two seconds makes people restless; millisecond-level jitter in robot control can cause collisions" — the two constraints together push all three scenarios toward the same architectural direction: from serial pipelines toward end-to-end models.
- "The end-to-end advantage lies mainly in **latency**; on the **accuracy** dimension it does not necessarily win."
- "Many times, data matters more than architecture."
- "If interactivity is to scale with intelligence, interactivity must become part of the model itself."
- "Whether fast/slow collaboration is useful depends on whether the task bottleneck is 'being able to think of it' or 'being able to react in time'."
- "There is no silver bullet in perception solutions — configure them following the model's temperament."
- "Reaching human-level accuracy does not equal being practical — **efficiency is the real bottleneck**."
- "Don't make the user wait in vain... use fast/slow decoupling to hide the 'slowness'" — fast/slow decoupling made voice responses about 15× faster, and "before the status summary confirms completion, you must never say 'it's done'."
- "Can generate video" ≠ "can guide robot actions"; the world model gives comparable predictions, not definite answers.
- "A prediction model can help rank candidate skills, but whether something is truly completed in the end still has to be confirmed through real observation and state checks."
